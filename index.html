Keeping AI-driven delivery accountable

AI drives the execution. People own the decisions. I built the layer that connects the two.

Problem
AI speeds up delivery, but speed doesn’t prove a team built the right thing. Requirements, risks, evidence, and approvals drift apart.
What I did
Applied the AWS AI-Driven Development Life Cycle (AI-DLC) to a real use case scenario, and built an AIDLC Command Center that keeps that work connected and checkable.
Proof
Milestone 1 accepted. 13 of 13 regression tests and 4 of 4 structure checks pass. One customer requirement traced from story to code to tests to a human gate.

How I applied AI-DLC

In AWS’s method, AI plans, asks clarifying questions, and builds only after people validate the direction. I added a named human gate at the end of each phase.

The Command Center

AWS says AI-DLC keeps persistent context in the project repository. This is my version: structured records in the repo, plus a validator that checks they hold together before anyone makes a decision.

Shared rules→ Each role + AI→ Command Center→ Human decision at G1, G2, G3
  • Execution plan. Work items and prerequisites, with every dependency checked for broken links and loops.
  • ROAM risk register. Every risk has an owner and a response.
  • KPI register. Planned targets kept separate from measured results.
  • Approval register. A record of human decisions, checked for completeness.
  • Python validator. Runs all of these checks, using only the standard library.

Decisions and trade-offs

Individual AI workspaces instead of AWS’s mob sessions

Distributed teams can’t always work together in real time, so each role uses its own AI tools and the Command Center becomes the shared space.

Trade-off: less live alignment, so the gate reviews have to carry the collaboration.

Named gates for every phase

AWS says AI defers critical decisions to people. Gates G1 to G3 turn that into decisions with written criteria and a record.

Trade-off: a deliberate review step at the end of every phase.

CSV records and standard-library Python

Lightweight, with no dependencies to install, and readable by anyone who opens the repository.

Trade-off: no way to verify who approved something, and no multi-user editing.

Operations left out of scope

Nothing was deployed in Milestone 1, and claiming an Operations phase would have overstated the work.

Trade-off: Gate G3 is defined but not yet tested in practice.

A human-centered AI-assisted product development life cycle

How people and AI can share the work of building products, and why people stay accountable for what gets built.

See the code on GitHub

The idea

AI speeds up the work. People decide what the work is for, use judgment along the way, and own the results.

AI can now draft requirements, code, tests, and documentation. What it can’t do is decide what matters most to a customer, build trust within a team, or take responsibility for a business decision.

So the point of bringing AI into product development isn’t just to do the same work faster. It’s to free up people’s time for the parts only they can do: understanding problems, working together, coming up with better ideas, and delivering something that genuinely helps.

What each side brings

Five areas where the split between AI and people matters most.

Understanding customers

AIAnalyzes requirements and customer feedback at scale.
PeopleRead the needs, feelings, and circumstances behind that feedback, and think through the consequences.

The result: solutions to real human problems.

Judgment

AISuggests options and points out possible gaps.
PeopleQuestion assumptions, weigh trade-offs, and make the call.

The result: better decisions, not just faster ones.

Working together

AITakes on repetitive work and fills in missing information.
PeopleUse the time saved to talk things through, settle disagreements, and get on the same page.

The result: stronger teams, not just busier individuals.

Creativity

AIHelps explore a wide range of possibilities quickly.
PeopleBring imagination, lived experience, domain knowledge, and the nerve to challenge how things are usually done.

The result: more room for creative problem-solving.

Responsibility and trust

AIProvides evidence through automated checks and analysis.
PeopleMake the ethical choices, decide which risks to accept, and answer for the consequences.

The result: technology people can understand, question, and trust.

This model puts people at the center of the whole life cycle, from defining the problem to judging whether the outcome was worth it.
